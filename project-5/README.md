This study bridges theoretical concepts and practical applications by analyzing option dynamics within the Black-Scholes-Merton (BSM) framework. Utilizing custom VBA scripts alongside real market data extracted from LSEG (Refinitiv) for Stellantis N.V., we investigated the "smoothing" effect of volatility shocks on the surfaces of the option Greeks. Empirically, the project reconstructs the implied volatility surface, clearly demonstrating that implied volatility is not constant across strikes and maturities, contrary to the core assumption of the Black-Scholes model. In particular, we observe a distinct downward concavity in the Put options' skew, a structural anomaly significantly influenced—though not exclusively caused—by the stock's substantial 10% dividend yield alongside broader market risk repricing.
